Sustainable Land Use

Why agroforestry?

Growing trees with crops and livestock on the same land gives farmers more income, healthier soil and a cooler, more resilient farm.

1,20,000 farmers enrolled in agroforestry carbon credit programmes
Core Definition

What is agroforestry?

Agroforestry means growing trees on farmland together with crops or livestock, instead of keeping trees and farming apart.

Trees can be planted on field boundaries, between crop rows, in orchards or in small blocks. They give timber, fruit, fodder and shade, and they protect the soil that crops depend on. Every tree also stores carbon, which is why agroforestry can earn carbon credits.

Triple Impact

Benefits of agroforestry

One practice that helps the farmer, the land and the climate.

👨‍🌾

For Farmers

  • More than one source of income: fruit, timber and crops
  • Lower risk when one crop or market fails
  • Fodder, fuelwood and shade on the farm
  • Long-term assets that grow in value each year
  • A route into carbon credit programmes
🌱

For Soil and Water

  • Leaf litter builds organic matter and soil life
  • Roots hold soil and reduce erosion by wind and water
  • Shade and mulch keep soil moist for longer
  • Nitrogen-fixing trees feed nearby crops
  • Better water infiltration in the rainy season
🌍

For Climate and Nature

  • Carbon stored in trunks, roots and soil
  • A cooler microclimate on hot days
  • Habitat for birds, bees and other pollinators
  • Windbreaks that protect crops and homes
  • Greener, more resilient landscapes
Field Layouts

Common agroforestry models

The right model depends on land size, water availability and local market demand.

01

Trees on Bunds and Boundaries

Trees along field edges take very little cropping space and add reliable timber income, shelter, and property demarcation.

02

Orchard with Intercrops

Fruit trees with vegetables or pulses grown between rows give short-term cash flow while the tree canopy matures.

03

Block Plantation

A dedicated small timber or pulpwood block (Teak, Melia dubia, Casuarina) on part of the farm for substantial long-term value.

04

Alley Cropping

Annual field crops cultivated in the alleys between regularly pruned hedgerows of nitrogen-fixing tree species.

05

Windbreaks & Shelterbelts

Linear tree rows that cut harsh wind speeds, reduce evaporative water loss, and protect tender crops from storms.

06

Trees with Livestock (Silvopasture)

Shade and protein-rich fodder trees (Subabul, Neem) integrated into grazing land that support cattle, sheep and goats.

Agro-Ecological Matching

Which trees for which land

General guidance. Our field team visits your plot to confirm the ideal species combination.

Dry and rainfed land
Custard apple, Amla, Neem, Pongamia
Coastal and sandy land
Casuarina, Coconut, Cashew, Sapota
Irrigated land with deep soil
Teak, Mango, Melia dubia, Bamboo, Lemon, Guava
Poor, rocky or sloping land
Cashew, Pongamia, Custard apple, Red sanders
Farm boundaries and bunds
Melia dubia, Bamboo, Neem, Subabul
