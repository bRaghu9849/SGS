Water Conservation & Low-Methane Farming

Alternate Wetting and Drying (AWD)

A simple way to grow paddy with less water and lower methane emissions.

5,000 farmers enrolled in AWD programmes
🌾
Field Water Tube Training SGS Officer demonstrating AWD in paddy field
Grassroots Water Technology

What is AWD?

Instead of keeping a paddy field flooded all the time, AWD lets the water level drop for some days before the field is irrigated again.

Continuously flooded paddy releases methane, a powerful greenhouse gas, because the waterlogged soil has no oxygen. The dry spells in AWD let air back into the soil, which cuts methane. They also reduce the number of irrigations a farmer needs.

Practical Field Guide

How AWD works in the field

Typical guidance. Our field team adapts it to your soil, variety and water source.

01

Set up a water tube

A perforated plastic pipe is pushed into the soil so the farmer can easily monitor the water level below the surface.

02

Flood, then let it dry

Keep the field flooded for 1–2 weeks after transplanting, then stop irrigating and let the water level naturally fall.

03

Irrigate at the mark

When the water level drops to about 15 cm below the soil surface in the tube, re-irrigate to 5 cm and repeat the cycle.

04

Keep water at flowering

Hold the field continuously flooded around flowering and heading, when the paddy crop needs water most.

Proven Results

Why farmers and the climate gain

Lower costs on the farm, lower emissions in the air.

👨‍🌾

For Farmers

  • Less irrigation water used per season (up to 30% water savings)
  • Lower pumping electricity and diesel costs where water is lifted by motor
  • Paddy yields are maintained when AWD is done according to protocol
  • Practical on-farm support and tube training from our field officers
  • A direct route into carbon credit programmes and revenue sharing
🌱

For the Environment

  • Significantly lower methane (CH4) emissions from flooded paddy soils
  • Conserves precious groundwater and canal water for downstream use
  • Less pressure on vulnerable aquifer systems and rural borewells
  • Healthier soil aeration and stronger root penetration from wetting/drying
  • A practical, low-cost climate action farmers can execute themselves

Is AWD right for my field?

AWD works where the farmer can start and stop irrigation and drain or hold water. Fields with unreliable water supply or heavy uncontrolled monsoon rain at key stages may need adjustments. Speak to our field team and we will check your field before you decide.

Our Role

What SGS does

We register farmers, train them in perforated tube monitoring, visit fields periodically, and keep clear records so results can be verified and reported to carbon registries and institutional partners.

AWD Practice & Monitoring

Follow approved water-management practices and maintain records through the crop season.

Payment to Farmer

The farmer’s share is calculated after applicable deductions and paid according to the written agreement. Ask for a clear payment statement.

Before joining, ask these 4 questions

  • What is the farmer’s share and how is it calculated?
  • Which costs or deductions may apply?
  • When is payment expected, and what conditions must be met?
  • Will the farmer receive a statement showing credits sold, price and payment?
